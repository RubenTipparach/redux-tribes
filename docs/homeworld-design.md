# Homeworld design language, for the Fallen Tribes fleet

What the owner asked for: ships designed by Homeworld's aesthetic. This is the
distilled reference, read out of *The Art of Homeworld* (Bluecanvas, 2014; the
concept work of Rob Cunningham and Aaron Kambeitz for Relic, with the
interviews that frame it) and written down as RULES this project can build
against, not as a description of the book. Nothing from the book is copied
here: the images stay where they are, and what follows is what they teach.

The book's own structure, for anyone going back to it: Homeworld 1 (Kushan,
Bentusi, Turanic Raiders, Kadeshi, Taiidan, backgrounds), Homeworld 2
(Hiigaran, Vaygr, Progenitors), animatics.

---

## 1. The two sentences that matter

**"What makes our fleets different from each other is where we place our
panel lines and our colours."** Not the silhouettes, not the technology, not
"one fleet is organic". Both Homeworld fleets are the same kind of thing,
built by humans, and the designers said so on purpose: the analogy they used
was the Russian and American navies, same universe, different habits. The
whole differentiation budget was spent on panel lines and paint.

**"Tasteful restraint."** No jellyfish aliens, no tentacle beasts, no magic.
Boats and railways were the colour reference. The temptation to do something
"really silly and high concept" was named and refused.

For this project that is a licence and a limit at once. The four navies
already share one lattice, one parts palette and one rasteriser. That is
Homeworld's own premise, and the work is not to give each navy a different
KIND of ship but a different way of panelling and painting the same kind.

## 2. Where the look comes from

The lineage the designers name, in their own order of importance:

- **Chris Foss**, and the imitators downstream of him, above all **Peter
  Elson**: the *Terran Trade Authority* handbooks. Airbrushed, colourful,
  paintings of a big machine in front of a dramatic background. Elson's own
  account of his panel lines: look at the painting, find a spot that looks
  bare, put a little something there.
- **Star Wars**: Ralph McQuarrie, Joe Johnston, Nilo Rodis-Jamero. What it
  gave them was CRUNCHINESS, MASS and cinematic value. The Hiigaran mothership
  went back to its "banana" profile because it read like a Star Destroyer,
  a shape the player already knew how to read as big.
- **Anime**, for Kambeitz.
- For scale and environment (Homeworld 2): **Richard Diebenkorn, John
  Harris, Syd Mead**. Mead is credited with the dynamic diagonal.
- **Ships and trains**: the red antifouling on the bottom of a tanker, a black
  hull with a white stripe along the top, railway liveries. That is where the
  Taiidan red and yellow and the Kushan navy, grey and white came from.

## 3. Two ways to draw a hull, and both are used

The book is explicit that the two artists worked in opposite ways, that the
fleets were split between them, and that the split IS the fleet identity.

**Enhance the form (Cunningham, the Kushan).** Colour identifies the natural
segments of a design. Panel lines reinforce the parts. Set up a broad flat
surface and frame it with lines that outline it. Colour is INFORMATION: on a
Kushan hull the yellow marks the business end of a utility component, orange a
heavy fitting or an antenna, brown the ceramic under-armour that never gets
painted. The player learns what a yellow bit does.

**Sabotage the form (Kambeitz, the Taiidan).** Set up a long flat surface and
cut panel lines ACROSS it. Put a colour stripe across it. The colours are the
"pageantry of battle": bold, blocky, red and yellow, meaning nothing except
boldness. The stripes wrap athwartships, so a long hull reads as a series of
bands rather than one plane.

The two cross-pollinated by the end, but the premise held: one fleet's panels
say what the parts are, the other's stripes cut the parts up.

## 4. Forms: what a Homeworld hull is made of

Read across a hundred sketches, the vocabulary is small and it repeats:

- **Chamfered boxes.** The basic mass is a rectangular block with its long
  edges taken off at 45 degrees, so a section is an octagon, never a circle or
  an ellipse. Corvettes are one such box; capital ships are several in a row.
- **Wedge prows.** The bow is a blade or a flat-fronted wedge, often with a
  flat face carrying a big emblem or numeral. Never a nose cone.
- **Stepped sterns and engine blocks.** The hull steps DOWN or narrows at the
  stern into a distinct engine block, and the bells sit INSIDE that block in a
  cluster (three in a row is the Kushan habit), with radiator vanes beside
  them. Engines are set into the hull, not hung off a transom.
- **Bridge tower aft, small.** Kushan capital ships carry a bridge block at
  the STERN, standing up off the deck, with antenna masts. It is one module
  among several and it sits at the back, never a slab along the deck.
- **Booms and pylons for guns, not for engines.** Gun pods and sensor pods
  sit on short stub pylons off the flanks (Taiidan corvettes, Kushan
  frigates). Long thin masts and rails (rail guns, antennae) run the length of
  the hull.
- **Hexagonal panel motif.** Kushan armour panels, hatches and turret bases
  are hexagons and octagons. Turrets are octagonal drums on a flat base plate,
  gun housing on top.
- **Long and low.** The Vaygr mothership is a horizontal stack of thin slabs,
  three times longer than it is tall. The Hiigaran carrier is a spine with the
  hangar and fittings hung along it. The ratio of length to depth on a capital
  ship is five to one and up.
- **Everything hangs off a spine.** A ship is a keel with modules along it,
  and the seams between the modules are the panel lines.
- **Asymmetry where honest.** One sensor dish on one side. Solar vanes on the
  Turanic pirate ships on articulated arms, unmatched. The Kushan research
  ship's arms unequal. Never decorative asymmetry, always a fitting that is
  only on one side because only one is needed.
- **Fins are plates.** Taiidan fighters and corvettes carry swept fin plates,
  thin and flat, painted with the stripe. They are never cylinders.

What is NOT in the vocabulary: saucers, domes, cylinders on pylons, warp
nacelles, anything organic, anything that reads as "space station" bolted to a
ship. The one rounded family is the Kadeshi swarmers (spherical pods with
claws) and the Bentusi (a rounded harbour ship), both of which are deliberate
outsiders.

## 5. Colour and stripes

- **A fleet is one base colour, one stripe colour, one accent.** Kushan: navy
  blue, grey and white with yellow accents. Taiidan: yellow with red stripes
  (the "poison fish"). Hiigaran: slate blue-grey with white chevrons and big
  white numerals, and an orange or yellow MODULE on a variant (capture
  corvette orange, minelayer yellow). Vaygr: dark grey with white bands and
  blue lights, or a striped orange and white "orangeship". Progenitors:
  olive drab with a pale keel line. Bentusi: bone white and gold.
- **Stripes are bands ACROSS the hull or along its waist**, painted like a
  tanker's boot topping or a railway carriage's cheat line. A stripe on a
  fin, a stripe around the engine block, a chevron on the prow.
- **Numerals and emblems.** Hiigaran hulls carry a two digit number the size
  of the flank and a squadron emblem. A ship is a numbered vehicle.
- **Colour is information (Kushan)** or **pageantry (Taiidan)**: pick one
  per navy and hold to it.
- **Lights are small and few**: running lights along an edge, a glow at the
  engine, a lit hangar mouth. No lit strips down the hull.
- **Materials are matte.** Painted metal, charcoal panels, ceramic
  under-armour. Nothing glossy, nothing chrome.

## 6. Scale and silhouette

- The scale ladder was pushed "to the absolute limits": fighter to mothership
  is hundreds of times. Every class must read as its own silhouette at map
  range: fighters are wedges with fins, corvettes are boxes, frigates are long
  boxes with a bridge, destroyers and cruisers are long boxes with gun rows,
  the mothership is a shape you already know.
- A capital ship's silhouette is a spine with a few large masses along it,
  read from the side. From above it is a long narrow wedge.
- Realism they wanted and never shipped: hulls flexing under manoeuvre like
  an aircraft carrier banking. What they did ship: mass, through slow motion
  and long shapes.

## 7. The Fallen Tribes navies, mapped

Same universe, four habits. Each navy keeps its authored SECTION ratio
(`NAVY_SECTION` in `web/src/app/design.ts`), because that ratio is what the
ladder and the sphere gates are built on; what changes is the section's
SHAPE, the panelling, the paint and the fittings.

| navy | Homeworld parent | method | section | paint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Terran | Kushan / Hiigaran | enhance the form | wide chamfered box, flat deck, chines | navy and grey plating, white cheat line, yellow on the working parts, a big numeral on the flank |
| Karisen | Taiidan | sabotage the form | tall chamfered box on a keel edge | yellow base, red bands athwartships, wrapped over the fins |
| Rogue | Turanic Raiders | neither: bolted together | broad low prism | patched dark hull, one red slash, the grapple tips lit; unmatched pods and a vane on an arm |
| Benefactor | Bentusi / Progenitor | enhance the form | tall hexagon on edge | bone and olive, a gold keel line, one tall fin |
| Civil | Kushan utility | information colour | plain box | grey hull, yellow working ends, hazard band at the waist |

The Terran deck rule the owner set is Homeworld's own: the Kushan bridge is a
SMALL block at the STERN with masts, and the deck forward of it is flat and
clear. A slab along the deck is not in the vocabulary, and neither is a
plateau painted as one.

## 8. What it means in the code, in order

1. **The section becomes a chamfered box.** `insideHull`, the rib cutter and
   the shell's inline ellipse test are one predicate; it gains a per navy
   exponent (a superellipse) and a chamfer. Half extents stay, so the ladder,
   the class radii and every socket seated by fraction stand.
2. **Panel lines come from the mesher, not from decor.** The greedy mesher
   merges runs of ONE colour into a quad, so a role boundary is a panel line.
   `roleOfCell` gains station based roles: a cheat line along the waist, a
   band athwartships at the module seams, a chevron on the prow, a numeral
   block on the flank. Terran roles follow the parts (enhance); Karisen roles
   cut across them (sabotage).
3. **The stern is a block.** The last sixth of the hull steps in and the
   drive sockets seat inside it; the transom is a flat plate with the bells
   set into it, and radiator vanes stand beside them as flat decor plates.
4. **Fittings are boxes and plates.** Fins are one course thick and swept.
   Pods are chamfered boxes on stub struts. Masts are one cell runs. Nothing
   round.
5. **Colour is information on the Terran and civil hulls**: the purpose palette
   already paints drives orange and guns red on every navy, which is exactly
   Kushan's rule, and the livery's accent role goes on the working ends.
6. **Numerals and emblems** are decals, the same mechanism as windows: a plate
   face wears a decal keyed by the hull's slot, so two frigates of a class
   carry different numbers.

Each of these is a separate change with its own mockup and its own
photographs, in that order: the section first, because everything else is
drawn on it.

## 9. What not to do, in one list

- No saucers, domes, nacelles, or cylinders on pylons.
- No slab, plateau or raised block along the top of a hull. A bridge is small
  and aft.
- No organic curves: every curve in the book is a chamfer or a bevel.
- No lit strips or glowing grilles. Lights are points and the engine glow.
- No gloss. Matte paint, charcoal panels, ceramic under-armour.
- No colour without a job. On a Kushan hull every colour tells the player
  something; on a Taiidan hull the stripes are the uniform. Neither is
  decoration.
- No two navies with the same panel habit. The panels ARE the navy.
