Exterior

Layers

Plate is authored one face at a time. Layers set what a face absorbs, and a gun with penetration is scored against fewer of them, so a belt that stops a beam is porous to a shell. The frame under it is not editable in either mode.

Paint

Armour only, and the colour you pick is the colour it stays: eight per faction, one hull. Nothing is chosen for you. Everything else on the ship is coloured by what it DOES, so a drive is orange and a gun is red whoever built them, which is what makes an unfamiliar hull readable without a legend. Cosmetic either way: never hashed, never sent to the core, so two players who painted the same hull differently cannot read as a desync.

Surfaces

A slot is a colour AND what it is made of, because the two are one decision: the same pale grey is a composite panel or bare rolled steel depending on the surface under it. Each swatch above carries its own, and "as hull" leaves it on whatever the hull wide finish is. The frame and the fitted parts are the inside of the ship, which a hole in the plating now shows, so they are choosable too. Cosmetic like the paint: never hashed, never sent to the core.

Draw armour

One slab at a time, on top of whatever the sliders built. Drag to paint a run. You are drawing plate and nothing else: the frame and the fitted parts are shown so you can work around them, and neither can be touched. Thick cuts the hull into deeper slabs rather than overlapping slices, so there are fewer of them to walk through and a run paints the whole depth. The slab you are standing in is boxed on the model. Mirror is off until you ask for it, and only x and y. A turret swings through its own box, hatched in red: nothing may be drawn there, and the generated exterior carves round it.

Hit volumes

Every shot goes into one of these. A volume soaks its own share of the damage and passes the rest through to the hull, so what is left of each one is what decides where the next hit lands.
