Reach precision

Three decisions about the movement envelope and the position picker. Every region on this page is probed from the real Rust core, compiled to wasm and inlined into this file, so what you see is the reachable set the game actually enforces.

The envelope has no closed form. It is a lobe along the nose, stretched by whatever velocity the ship is already carrying, so the client cannot draw it from a radius and has to ask the core one point at a time. Everything below is about how many times to ask, and where to put the answer.

ship probing
Terran Frigate, the shipped numbers: yaw 6.0/s, pitch 4.0/s, thrust 0.9 fwd / 0.35 retro / 0.25 lat, max speed 8.0. Tolerance 1.6 units, 60 probe steps. All from data.rs and types.ts.

1. The boundary line

Marching squares walks a grid and joins edge midpoints. The midpoint is not where the boundary is: it can sit up to half a cell off, which is what makes the outline faceted. Bisecting each crossing edge against the core puts the vertex on the real boundary instead, for about two extra probes per vertex.

2. The volume

Today the 3D shell is 14 cells a side, 2744 probes, drawn as loose dots. Dots at that spacing read as a cloud rather than a shape, and a grid spends most of its probes deep inside the region where the answer was never in doubt.

Disc integration spends them on the boundary instead. Slice the region by altitude, and on each slice cast rays outward from an interior point, bisecting each one onto the surface. Every probe lands on the boundary, each slice comes back as a closed polygon that can be filled and lofted to the next, and the stack integrates to an actual volume by the disc method: the cross sectional area of each slice times its thickness, summed.

It rests on the region being star shaped about the centre, so that a ray crosses the surface once. That is checked below rather than assumed, and any ray that crosses more than once is reported.

Measured: disc integration is not always valid here. Ray casting is exact only if the region is star shaped about the seed, meaning every ray crosses the surface once. Sweeping the shipped flight model over three move modes, three speeds and two headings, sampling each ray at 240 points rather than peeking just past the first crossing:

mode speed rays multi-crossing understated by ------------ ----- ---- -------------- -------------- MoveAndTurn 0 24 0 0.0 u MoveAndTurn 4 24 2 5.0 u TurnSlide 0 24 0 0.0 u TurnSlide 4 24 0 0.0 u

Why it fails, and it is not a rounding problem. A ship carrying speed cannot stop where it already is, so the reachable set has a hole punched around the ship. A region with a hole is star shaped about no point at all: a ray aimed across the hole leaves the set and comes back, which is the two crossings above. At 4 units per second that costs 5 units of real reach, drawn as unreachable.

So it is a drawing technique, never the rule. The picker asks ft_can_reach for the exact point under the cursor and always will, so a shape drawn slightly small can never authorise or refuse a move by itself. Use the discs for the look and for a volume figure; keep the contour for the boundary you click against.

3. The picker at the edge

Drag inside the shape. Today a drag that leaves the reachable set is simply refused, so the marker stops dead at the last point that was real and the plan stops tracking your hand. Bisecting from the ship toward the cursor slides the marker along the boundary instead: it keeps following you, and it lands exactly on the edge rather than a grid cell away from it.

What is being decided

1. Bisect the contour vertices? Yes gives a smooth outline that matches the rule exactly. It costs about two probes per boundary vertex, measured below.

2. Draw the volume as stacked contours or discs instead of dots? Same order of probes either way, so this is a look decision. Discs additionally give a volume figure, at the cost of understating the shape in the states measured above.

3. Slide the picker along the boundary instead of freezing? This changes how planning feels at the edge, which is where most of the interesting orders are given.

If none of these is right, the fallback is to raise the grid resolution instead, which costs probes cubically for the volume and quadratically for the contour, and still lands vertices on midpoints.
