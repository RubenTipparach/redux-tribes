How a cell is picked

A tap on a 2D screen names a ray, not a point, so an empty lattice has no single answer. Two rules remove the ambiguity, and both are visible:

1. The slab. Exactly one layer is live at a time. The ray is intersected with that one plane, so the depth is chosen by you, not guessed. Off slab cells are drawn faint and cannot be picked.

2. Faces. A tap on a placed cube takes the cell across the face you touched, which is exact at any angle, and the slab snaps to it.

Nearest wins. Slab and cube are both solid, so a tap resolves to whichever is closer to you. A cube behind the slab cannot steal a tap, and the slab cannot be reached through a cube in front of it.

Then aim, then commit: the first tap only moves the cursor and shows a ghost with its cost, a second tap on the same cell or the big button commits. A misjudged tap costs nothing.

When the slab is close to edge on the ray barely crosses it, so the pick is refused and the hint says so rather than dropping a cube somewhere plausible.
