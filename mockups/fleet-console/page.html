Fleet Rail and Ship Data

The console could tell you a hull was hurt. It could not tell you where, and where is the whole question: a shot goes into one volume, and which volume decides whether the ship stops turning, stops shooting, or just loses paint. Four readouts, one subject.

Every number on this page is read out of engine/sim_core/src/data.rs (frigate_subs) and engine/sim_core/src/turn.rs, so the volumes below sit where the damage model actually puts them and soak what it actually takes off. The schematic is drawn to scale from those offsets and radii against a 3.5 unit hull radius.

1The fleet rail, as chips

A chip is a button with a picture of the ship on it: a player who spent an hour in the shipyard should be able to find their hull without reading. What is broken sits beside it rather than inside it, because a chip listing all six volumes every turn is a chip nobody reads, and the two that matter are the two that are out. Targeting is per ship, so both rails carry it: your chip says who it is aiming at, and the hostile chip says which of yours are on it.

The shipyard's view of a hull with the live damage model over it, so the thing a player designed and the thing being shot at are looked at the same way. Hover a sphere or a row; both light the other. Every sphere is drawn at the radius the core reports, because how much of a hull one covers is exactly what a player is deciding about when they aim at it.

3Ship data, out on the map

The same volumes, labelled where they are. Offered rather than always on: the labels are useful at the zoom where a hull fills a third of the screen and are a fog of text at the zoom a match is planned at, so the button only appears once the camera is actually looking at a ship, and the mode lets go the moment that stops being true. The sentence about what losing a volume costs belongs to the one being pointed at, not to all six at once.

jets offline Soaks 40% of what reaches it. Attitude jets. When the last one goes the drive still pushes and the hull stops swinging, so it re-flies the rest of the turn on an envelope with no turn left in it.

4The camera gestures that must never touch a plan

Left drag pans when it starts outside the reachable area, which means the gesture that moves the camera is also the gesture that moves a ship, and near your own hull the difference is a few pixels. So there is a gesture on each device that cannot be wrong: it is decided before anything reads the world, so it never picks a ship, never grabs the heading ring, and never drops a destination.
