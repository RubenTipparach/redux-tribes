Homeworld fleet direction

The Star Trek dress came off. The owner wants the ships designed by Homeworld's aesthetic instead: faceted hulls with hard chines, long spines, stepped sterns with exposed engine blocks, radiator fins, and a striped livery that reads at map range. This page is the direction, sketched on the real sections, before a hull is cut. Approve the direction and the navies get built; say which navy reads wrong and it is redrawn here first.

01What Homeworld looks like, in five words

Faceted, not rounded. Homeworld hulls are wedges and prisms: a flat deck meeting a flat flank on a hard chine, a prow that is a blade rather than a nose, a stern that steps down to a block of engine bells with radiator fins standing off it. Long. Every capital ship is a spine with modules bolted along it, so a hull reads as several boxes in a row rather than one lozenge. Striped: one base colour, one stripe colour, laid as a few long bands and a chevron on the prow, which is how a fleet is told apart at the distance the game is played at. Asymmetric where it is honest: a sensor mast on one side, a hangar mouth on the other. And nothing organic: no domes, no saucers, no cylinders on pylons.

The reference. docs/homeworld-design.md is the language read out of The Art of Homeworld: the two panelling methods (enhance the form, sabotage the form), colour as information against colour as pageantry, the forms (chamfered boxes, wedge prows, stepped sterns with engine blocks, small bridges aft, plate fins) and the navy mapping this page draws. The page is the picture; the document is why.
Why the current fleet cannot get there by decor alone. Every hull is an ELLIPSE at every station: the shell, the ribs and the seating all use one insideHull test with the same ellipse. Bolting facets onto a lozenge is a lozenge with facets stuck on. The section itself has to become a faceted one, which is a change to one predicate rather than to seventeen frames. The slab the owner pointed at on the Terran cruiser was this ellipse: wide and flat, its top twelve cells across quantises to a plateau, and painted as deck it is a block on top of the ship whatever colour it is.

02The section: a chamfered box, per navy

One function replaces the ellipse. A station is a superellipse |x/a|^n + |y/b|^n ≤ 1 with a per navy exponent, plus an optional chamfer: Terran squarish with wide chamfers (a flat deck narrower than the beam, meeting the flank on a chine), Karisen a taller diamond, Rogue a broad low prism, Benefactor a tall hexagon, civil a plain box. The half extents stay what NAVY_SECTION says, so the ladder and every sphere gate stand.

04What it costs, and what it does not touch

  • One predicate. insideHull, the rib cutter and the shell's inline ellipse test become one superellipse test with the navy's exponent and chamfer. The sphere gates, the ladder, the class radii and the sockets seated by fraction stand: a chamfered box at the same half extents fits the same sphere.
  • The four frigates' raw sockets are the risk: they were read off elliptical archive hulls in cells. A corner that the ellipse rounded off and the box keeps is room gained, not lost, so an enclosed part stays inside; the seating check in sim.test.mjs is what says so.
  • Livery: roleOfCell gains the stripe and the chevron as two roles laid by station rather than by height. The permutation rule stands, eight swatches on every hull.
  • Decor: fins as flat plates, engine blocks as stepped sterns, pods as boxes. All plate, all against the skin, all clear of the rings by the same arc scan.
  • Nothing crosses the boundary. The core still derives from plate counts, extents and mount positions; a faceted hull is a different count, re-measured by measure_fleet.mjs --sync.

What was decided here. The direction: faceted sections per navy, stepped sterns with engine blocks, flat fins, a striped livery, and the navy to Homeworld mapping above (Terran as Hiigaran, Karisen as Vaygr, Rogue as Turanic, Benefactor as Bentusi). Approved, the section predicate is built first and photographed on all twenty three hulls before any decor is cut. Not approved, the fleet stays as it is on this branch: nacelles off, decks clear, windows on every flank.
