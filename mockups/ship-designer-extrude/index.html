How it builds

Tap the exposed face of a cube and a new one grows against it. There is no lattice and no empty cell to hit, so nothing can float: every cube arrives touching the structure. Long press a cube, or switch to DEL, to take one away.

Deleting is the one move that can break the rule placing cannot: cut the spine and the nose becomes an island. The core catches that, the editor does not prevent it.

Reaching a face you cannot see

Three routes, in the order they cost you least.

1. Turn it. Most occluded faces are outer faces. Drag empty space, or hold TURN, and orbit until the face is front on.

2. Slice it. SLICE hides everything between the camera and a cut plane and takes it out of the pick as well, so a buried face becomes the front face and one tap builds on it. The hidden cubes stay in the design and are drawn as faint outlines. Minus and plus walk the plane through the hull, and the side is chosen from where the camera is standing, so there is no side control to get wrong.

3. Peel it. DEL takes the cube in the way off, build behind it, put it back. Destructive, and the only route that needs undo.

Tap through, where a second tap on the same pixel steps to the next surface along the ray, was tried and dropped: it makes every placement two taps to save one, and this variant is spending its budget on flow.

Read from the core

Every number below comes back from one call, ft_read_design(ship). The editor owns which cell a finger landed on and what a cube looks like. It computes no mass, no thrust, no legality: a rule with a second implementation in the client is a rule two clients can disagree about, and that is a desync.
